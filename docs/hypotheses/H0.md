# Hypothesis 0: Structured Action Representation

## Motivation

Ctrl-World uses an MLP-based action encoder. A conventional MLP may not
explicitly capture the geometric structure and temporal dependencies of
continuous robot actions.

## Hypothesis

**H0:** A structured action encoding that captures the geometric and
temporal properties of robot actions can improve the quality,
controllability, and action consistency of action-conditioned world
models compared with a conventional MLP action encoder.

## Proposed Experiments

-   **E0 --- Baseline:** Original MLP action encoder.
-   **E1 --- Fourier-enhanced MLP:** Add Fourier features to represent
    continuous action variations at multiple scales.
-   **E2 --- Structured encoder:** Encode translation, rotation, and
    gripper actions separately, then fuse their representations.
-   **E3 --- Temporal encoder:** Encode action sequences using a
    lightweight temporal model (e.g., Conv1D).
-   **E4 --- Combined encoder:** Combine structured and temporal
    encoding.

## Training Plan

Reuse the pretrained Ctrl-World checkpoint where possible. Train the
newly introduced encoder components and fine-tune relevant
action-conditioning/downstream layers; compare freezing versus
controlled fine-tuning of the pretrained backbone. Avoid full retraining
from scratch unless necessary.

## Evaluation

Compare against the original encoder using video-generation quality,
action-following/controllability, temporal consistency, and task success
(where available). Keep the data, model, training schedule, and
evaluation setup as controlled as possible.

## Initial Priority

Start with **E1 (Fourier-enhanced MLP)** and **E2 (Structured encoder)**
as relatively lightweight, informative experiments.
